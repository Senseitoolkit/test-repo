The five-finger check

  1. Open the book to a page in the middle that is mostly words.
  2. Read the page. Put up one finger for every word you do not know.
  3. Count your fingers at the end of the page.
0–1Easy read
2–3Just right
4A stretch
5+Not yet

The movie test. After a page, can you picture what happened? If your mind is blank, the words may be too tricky for now.

The do-I-care test. Do you want to know what happens next? A book at the perfect level is still the wrong book if it bores you.

Putting a hard book back means not yet. You can come back to it.

What the spine colours mean

Quick & Fun. Lots of pictures and fast pages. Great for getting hooked on reading. Mix in other kinds too.
Just Right. A good match for the grade. The best place to spend most reading time.
Reach Higher. Longer or harder. Try it when you feel brave, or read it with a buddy or a grown-up.

Reading levels

For teachers and families

Grade groupings are suggested starting points. A Grade 2 reader may love a Grade 4 series and the other way round, so use the grade buttons as a way in.

Reading levels describe the general reading experience. They are not Lexile or Accelerated Reader levels, and single titles inside a series vary.

Tap any series to open its box. Inside are two kinds of heads-up. Reading heads-up is about how the book reads: length, vocabulary, layout. What's in it covers content such as scary scenes, toilet humour, loss or war, and is listed for the most popular series.

The shows every series in one sortable table that you can copy into a spreadsheet.

Nothing here needs a login. Each reader's list stays in their own browser.
