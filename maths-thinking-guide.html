Routine · the maths thinking routines

Fourteen routines that make mathematical thinking visible

These are the routines referenced on every objective card. Each one is short, repeatable and works from Reception to Year 6 with the content changed. Pick four and use them weekly; the rest are there when the content calls for them.

How to make a routine stick. Name it, display its steps, run it the same way every time, and hand it over: by the third week a student should be able to lead "What makes you say that?" or run a Number Talk on the board. A routine students can name is a routine they will reach for in a test.

Support · the two papers

Teaching students to think inside the paper

Paper 1 is arithmetic: fluency, accuracy and time. Paper 2 is reasoning and problem solving: reading, representing, deciding and explaining. They need different routines, and both need to be practised with the paper in front of the student, not just the content.

Paper 1 · Arithmetic 20–25 minutes from Year 3; no time limit in Years 1–2
1
Before

Fast, slow, check

Teach students to go through the paper three times. Fast pass: answer everything you can do in your head (bonds, tables, × 10, known facts). Slow pass: the written methods, each one estimated first. Check pass: re-read the question for every box, especially the missing-number ones where the box is not at the end.

2
During

Estimate before every written method

A two-second estimate (712 × 39 is about 700 × 40 = 28,000) catches the misplaced zero that loses the mark. Write the estimate in the margin. Method marks on 2-mark questions reward a correct layout even with one slip, so set the work out clearly.

3
Watch

The questions that catch students

  • Equations with the box on the left or in the middle (□ = 38 + 16; 10 + 9 = 18 + □): the = sign means balance.
  • × 0, × 1, ÷ 1: students overthink them.
  • Order of operations (2 × 7 + 8 × 7) in Year 6.
  • Fractions past one, and mixed-number subtraction.
  • Subtracting from a whole number with zeros (4,008 − 2,394; 5 − 1.6).
  • Units written in the answer (20 cm × 4 = 80 cm).
4
After

Mark it as data

Sort errors into three piles: didn't know the fact, knew it but slipped, misread the question. Each pile has a different fix (retrieval practice, estimate-and-check habit, reading routine). Students keep their own tally across the term.

Paper 2 · Reasoning and problem solving 35–50 minutes from Year 3
1
Read

Read it twice, then draw it

First read for the story, second read for the numbers and the question. Underline the question. Then represent it before calculating: a bar model, a number line, a quick sketch, a table. The drawing is where the operation becomes obvious.

2
Decide

Name the type of problem

Students should be able to say "this is a difference problem", "this is a fraction of an amount running backwards", "this is a scale factor". Naming the type unlocks the routine for it. Interleaved practice (one of each type in a starter) builds this faster than topic-by-topic practice.

3
Explain

"Do you agree? Explain." is a three-part answer

Say the verdict. Show the correct working or fact. Point to exactly where the other person went wrong. The mark scheme awards the mark only with the explanation, so students practise writing these in two sentences with the stems on the card.

4
Check

Does the answer fit the story?

22 buses, not 21.075. £2.50, not £2.5. 5,830 metres when the question asked for metres. The last ten seconds of every question go to "is this a sensible answer to the question that was asked, in the unit it asked for?"

Reception to Year 2

Papers

No time limit. Questions are read with an adult where needed. The reasoning paper includes drawing, circling and tapping tasks, so practise the formats: 'circle two numbers that make 10', 'draw an arrow to 18', 'tick the shape'. Model saying the thinking aloud: "I need two numbers. I'll try 8… 8 and 2."

Years 3 to 4

Papers

Timed papers begin. Teach pacing with a visible clock: about a minute a question on Paper 1, two to three on Paper 2. The jump is the two-step word problem and the first 'explain' questions. Bar models should be automatic by the end of Year 3.

Years 5 to 6

Papers

Two-mark method questions appear in arithmetic (long multiplication, long division). Reasoning questions chain three steps and expect fractions, decimals and percentages to be swapped freely. Students should choose the representation, not wait to be told it.
