1. The diploma in one minute

The IB Diploma is a two-year programme, usually taken from age 16 to 18. A student takes six subjects and completes three extra requirements called the core. The final result is one number out of 45.

  • Six subjects, each graded 1 to 7. A 7 is the top grade. Six subjects give up to 42 points.
  • Three subjects are taken at Higher Level and three at Standard Level. Higher Level covers more content. Both levels are graded 1 to 7 and count the same number of points.
  • The core adds up to 3 points. These come from a long research essay (the Extended Essay) and a course about knowledge (Theory of Knowledge).
  • A third core part, CAS, earns no points but is compulsory. It covers creative, physical and community activities.
  • Grades come from two sources. Final exams at the end of year 2 carry most of the weight. Coursework done during the two years carries the rest.
For parentsYou do not need to know the syllabus. Your part is to know the calendar, keep routines steady, and notice early when something slips. Sections 6 to 8 are written for that.
For studentsThe outcome is yours to control. Know exactly where each point comes from, then plan the two years backwards from the exams.
For teachersYou set the pace on coursework and feedback. Clear deadlines and honest predicted grades are the strongest levers you hold. This page is also a ready explainer for parents' evenings.

2. What the scores mean

A score of 40 or more puts a student in roughly the top 1 in 20 worldwide. It is a demanding target, and plenty of excellent university offers sit below it.

3. Words you will hear

Schools use a lot of abbreviations. These are the ones that matter for the final score.

HL and SL
Higher Level and Standard Level. Three subjects at each. HL has more content and harder exams.
The core
The three requirements on top of the six subjects: TOK, the EE and CAS.
TOK (Theory of Knowledge)
A course on how we know what we know. Graded A to E from an essay and an exhibition.
EE (Extended Essay)
An independent research essay of up to 4,000 words. Graded A to E.
CAS
Creativity, Activity, Service. Ongoing activities outside lessons. No grade, but the diploma is not awarded without it.
IA (Internal Assessment)
Coursework in each subject, such as a lab report, project or oral exam. Marked by the teacher and checked by the IB.
Predicted grades
The teacher's forecast of each final grade. Universities use these when deciding on offers.
Mock exams
Practice exams set by the school in year 2. The best preview of the real result.
Markscheme
The official marking guide for an exam paper. It shows exactly which answers earn marks.
Grade boundaries
The marks needed for each grade. They change slightly at every exam session.
IB coordinator
The staff member who runs the diploma at the school. Your first contact for deadlines and rules.
Exam session
Exams are held in May or November, depending on the school. May results arrive in early July.

    7. Warning signs and what to do

    Most missed targets are visible months ahead. These are the signs worth acting on early.

    What you noticeWhy it mattersWhat to do
    A subject at grade 5 or below after year 1 examsEach 5 needs a 7 elsewhere to stay on 40Meet that teacher and ask for the three biggest gaps
    No clear essay question by the end of year 1The core points are at risk, and the essay will collide with examsBook a meeting with the essay supervisor this month
    Coursework deadlines being missed or movedWork piles into the final term and eats revision timePut every deadline on one calendar and work backwards
    Revision means rereading notesExams reward practised answers, and rereading does not build themSwitch to timed past papers marked with the markscheme
    Little or nothing logged for CASAn incomplete CAS means no diploma, whatever the scoreSpeak to the CAS coordinator and agree a catch-up plan

    8. Questions to ask

    Ask the school

    • What is the current predicted grade in each subject?
    • What exactly would move this subject from a 6 to a 7?
    • What are the internal deadlines for coursework, the essay and TOK?
    • Is the Extended Essay on track for an A or B?
    • Is CAS on track to be completed?
    • What extra support does the school offer before exams?

    Ask your child

    • Which subject feels least under control right now?
    • What is due in the next four weeks?
    • When did you last do a timed past paper, and how did it go?
    • What would help most from me this month?

    Ask about the plan and the workload more often than about the grades.

    Ask your teachers

    • What is my current grade, and how many marks am I from a 7?
    • Which criteria is my coursework losing marks on?
    • Which exam paper or question type costs me the most marks?
    • Can I see a top-band example of this coursework?
    • What would raise my predicted grade before applications go in?

    Ask yourself each month

    • Which subject is furthest from its target grade?
    • What is due in the next four weeks?
    • How many timed past papers did I do this month?
    • Am I sleeping enough to keep this up?

    Ask your students

    • Show me your error log. What mistake keeps coming back?
    • Which paper costs you the most marks, and why?
    • What is your plan and date for the next coursework draft?
    • Which syllabus points can you not yet explain from memory?

    Tell parents clearly

    • The current predicted grade and what it is based on
    • The one or two changes that would raise it
    • Every internal deadline for the year, in one list
    • Who to contact first when something slips

    9. Where the points are won

    Choosing subjects and levels

    • Check university course requirements first. Some courses require specific Higher Level subjects.
    • Put the three strongest subjects at Higher Level. Choose on strength, never on prestige.
    • A 7 at Standard Level counts the same 7 points, so these are often the most reachable 7s.
    • Ask the school for its past results by subject.

    The core (up to 3 points)

    • Aim for an A and a B across the essay and TOK. That gives all 3 points.
    • Write the essay in a strong subject, on a narrow question, fixed early.
    • Draft the full essay in the break between year 1 and year 2.
    • In TOK, answer the set title exactly, using specific real examples.
    • Log CAS activities as they happen.

    Coursework (IAs)

    • Coursework is typically around 20 to 30% of a subject grade (approximate; it varies by subject).
    • It is done with time and feedback, so it is the safest place to earn marks.
    • Work through the marking criteria line by line.
    • Hand in a complete draft, since teachers may only give feedback once.

    Exams

    • Use the syllabus as a checklist and fix the weakest topics first.
    • Do past papers by topic in year 1 and as full timed papers in year 2.
    • Mark answers with the official markscheme.
    • Keep a log of mistakes and redo those questions weeks later.
    • Protect sleep. Exams run for about three weeks.

    10. Common myths

    "A 7 at Higher Level is worth more points."False. A 7 is 7 points at either level. Universities may ask for certain grades in HL subjects, but the diploma total treats them the same.
    "You need all 7s to reach 40."False. With all 3 core points, one 7 and five 6s make 40.
    "The essay and TOK are side projects."False. They decide up to 3 points, and an E in either one means no diploma.
    "CAS adds to the score."False. CAS earns no points. It is still compulsory for the diploma.
    "Only the final exams count."False. Coursework is part of every subject grade and is finished before exams begin.
    "Year 1 does not matter much."False. Final exams cover both years, and predicted grades for university offers rest on year 1 work.
