      Guide to 40+

      How the diploma is scored, how to choose subjects, and where the points are won and lost.

      How the IB Diploma works

      You take six subjects, one from each group, with three at Higher Level (HL) and three at Standard Level (SL). HL goes into more depth and has more teaching hours.

      GroupWhat it covers
      1. Studies in Language and LiteratureYour strongest language
      2. Language AcquisitionA second language
      3. Individuals and SocietiesHistory, economics, psychology and similar
      4. SciencesBiology, chemistry, physics and similar
      5. MathematicsMathematics
      6. The ArtsAn arts subject, or a second subject from groups 1 to 4

      The core

      • Theory of Knowledge (TOK): a course on how we know what we know, assessed by an essay and an exhibition. Open the TOK workbench
      • Extended Essay (EE): an independent research paper of up to 4,000 words on a topic you choose. Open the EE planner
      • Creativity, Activity, Service (CAS): ongoing projects in arts, physical activity and community service. It isn't graded, but you must complete it. Open your CAS log

      Scoring

      • Each subject is graded 1 to 7, for a maximum of 42 points.
      • TOK and the EE together add up to 3 points, making the total 45.
      • You need 24 points for the diploma, plus minimum conditions such as completing CAS.
      • Grades combine final exams (May or November) with internal assessments such as lab reports, oral exams and projects.

      Choosing subjects and levels

      Pick the combination where you can realistically reach a 6 or 7 in all six, within what your target universities require.

      1. Check university requirements first. Medicine, engineering and economics courses often require specific HL subjects, such as HL Chemistry or HL Mathematics.
      2. Put your three strongest subjects at HL. A 7 at HL depends on real strength and interest. Avoid a fourth HL unless a university asks for it.
      3. Use SL for your weaker areas. A 7 at SL counts the same 7 points as a 7 at HL.
      4. Choose your Group 2 level honestly. A language you already speak well is a likely 7. A beginner (ab initio) course is SL only.
      5. Pick the right mathematics course. Analysis and Approaches is more abstract and algebraic; Applications and Interpretation is more statistical and applied.
      6. Use Group 6 deliberately. Take an arts subject if you are strong in it. Otherwise choose a second science or humanities subject you are good at.
      7. Ask your school for its own results. Past grade distributions by subject and teacher tell you more than global averages.

      Securing the core points

      Target an A and a B across TOK and the EE. An A and a B, in either order, gives all 3 points, and two Bs still give 2.

      Extended Essay

      • Write it in a subject you take, ideally at HL, on a topic narrow enough for 4,000 words.
      • Fix a sharp research question early. Vague questions stall at a C.
      • Read the assessment criteria before each draft.
      • Finish a full draft in the break between year 1 and year 2.
      • Take the three supervisor reflection sessions seriously; your reflection is assessed. Plan the EE

      Theory of Knowledge

      • The essay carries about two-thirds of the grade, the exhibition about one-third.
      • Answer the prescribed title exactly as worded, with specific real examples and a clear counter-argument. Plan the essay
      • For the exhibition, choose three concrete objects with a specific context and link each to the prompt. Plan the exhibition

      CAS

      • CAS earns no points, but an incomplete CAS means no diploma.
      • Log experiences and reflections as you go. Log an experience

      Coursework and exams

      Internal assessments

      • IAs typically count for roughly 20 to 30% of a subject grade (approximate; check each subject guide).
      • A top-band IA lowers the exam mark you need for a 7.
      • Work from the marking criteria line by line.
      • Read high-scoring sample IAs before choosing a topic.
      • Use your one round of teacher feedback on a complete draft.
      • Spread the deadlines. IAs, the EE and the TOK essay often land in the same months.

      Study and exam strategy

      • Use the syllabus as your checklist and work on the weakest points first.
      • Start past papers early: by topic in year 1, full timed papers in year 2.
      • Mark with the markscheme and learn the command terms.
      • Keep an error log and redo those questions a few weeks later.
      • Revise actively. Recall and spaced repetition beat rereading.
      • Know your boundaries and aim several marks above the 7 line.
      • Protect sleep and exercise. Exams run over about three weeks.

      Common ways 40+ slips away

      • One weak subject. A single 5 means you need a 7 elsewhere to offset it.
      • Neglecting the core. Settling for 1 core point raises the bar to three 7s.
      • Late coursework. IAs and the EE finished in the final term eat into revision.
      • HL overload. Choosing HLs for prestige over strength turns likely 7s into 5s and 6s.
      • Passive revision. Rereading notes does not prepare you for markscheme-style answers.
      • Ignoring SL subjects. SL 7s are usually the most achievable points available.
